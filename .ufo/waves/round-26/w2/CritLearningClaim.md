9. [obs][inf] The SH3 dup-on-SSD caveat is generic and misses this host's LUKS layer. The caveat says "an SSD may store both as one physical copy", based on S130 mkfs.btrfs.rst lines 638-641 ("a SSD drive can remap the blocks internally to a single copy--thus deduplicating them"). The entry's Limits section says "Whether the SSD deduplicates the `dup` metadata copies is not observable from the host" (`storage-health-r26.md:113`). But on this host the btrfs device is `/dev/mapper/root`, a dm-crypt mapping on `nvme0n1p2` (capture lines 32-33). Linux v7.2 `drivers/md/dm-crypt.c:312-313` says "plain64: the initial vector is the 64-bit little-endian version of the sector number" (fetched from https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/v7.2/drivers/md/dm-crypt.c, HTTP 200, sha256 `1f13e70c…18ef3`, copy under `/fast/scratch/hyaku/tsuku26/w2/CritLearningClaim/`). [inf] With a per-sector IV, the two dup copies at different sectors encrypt to different ciphertext, so the SSD sees two different blocks and cannot merge them by content. On this host the dedup caveat is therefore likely void, which is a real host-specific refinement the entry missed. [obs] `/proc/crypto` lists `xts(aes)` with drivers `xts-aes-aesni`, `xts-aes-vaes-avx2` and others, which is consistent with `aes-xts-plain64`. [unk] The actual cipher and IV mode of the `root` mapping were not read (it needs `cryptsetup status` or the LUKS header, neither of which is on the allow-list). Smallest correction: add the IV mode to the capture next round and, if it is plain64 or essiv, narrow the SH3 caveat and the Limits line. Impacted axis: depth.
