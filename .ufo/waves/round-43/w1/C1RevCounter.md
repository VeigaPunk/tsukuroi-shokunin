DEFECTS

D1 (minor, cannot flip toward PASS) — float mtime in the live oracle. score-bootinputs-r43.py:224 and :230 use `st.st_mtime > bt` (float) where GNU find compares nanosecond timespecs for `-newermt @bt`. float64 spacing at btime-scale values (~1.79e9) is ~2.4e-7 s, so a live boot-chain input whose mtime lies within ~±120 ns above btime can round to exactly bt in the oracle ("0") while find reports "1". Effect: live_agree=0 → verdict FAIL on a good revision — a false negative only. No construct exists whereby the oracle says "1" while find says "0" (rounding up past bt requires the true ns value to already exceed bt). Not observed: 3 runs on this host, live_agree=1 each. One-line hardening: compare `st.st_mtime_ns > bt * 10**9`.

D2 (minor, no predicate effect) — on SIGINT/SIGTERM the counter removes its scratch but does not terminate in-flight children. main() installs handlers that raise SystemExit (:247-251) and a `finally: shutil.rmtree(work)` (:252-255); Python's subprocess.run kills a child only on TimeoutExpired, so an interrupt during the ns leg (:295), live leg (:314) or selftest leg (:324) can orphan that child until its own timeout. Empirically bounded: 7 interrupts (TERM at t=0.15/0.20/0.25/0.40/0.45 s, INT at 0.35 s of a 0.64 s run) → rc 143/130 every time, zero leftover files under --scratch every time (the finally demonstrably ran — a default-disposition kill would leave the work dir), `ps` children-of-counter at kill time empty in all 3 probed trials, zero leftover processes, and no `r43` mounts ever visible on the host afterwards (all writes of the orphaned legs are confined to the private mount ns or to the already-removed work dir). Receipt: C1RevCounter/sigtest-summary.txt.
