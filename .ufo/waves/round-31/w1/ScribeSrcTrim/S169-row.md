Asynchronous trim is an improved version of synchronous trim, enabled with -o discard=async; freed extents are first tracked in memory, and trim starts after a period or when enough ranges accumulate, expecting larger ranges and permitting throttling of I/O requests so the rest of filesystem activity is not interfered with. Manual fstrim starts trimming the whole filesystem without mount options; the filesystem traverses free space and starts trim, making this suitable for a periodic service. Trim is a device hint: a device may ignore it, process only ranges meeting criteria, or skip it due to other factors affecting memory cells. Internal data relocation may cause unexpected performance drops, and periodic trim could prevent that too.
